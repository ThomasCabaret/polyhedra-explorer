Provenance

Geometry data sources and attribution

The viewer stores published vertex coordinates and face-boundary data as readable JavaScript arrays. The goal is to keep the visualization auditable rather than hiding the geometry inside opaque mesh files.

Szilassi polyhedron

  • Lajos Szilassi, Regular Toroids, Structural Topology 13 (1986), pp. 69-80. The polyhedron was discovered in 1977. Open reference.
  • The coordinate realization used here is derived from the Python source published with Szilassi polyhedron.stl by Wikimedia Commons user Cmglee. Open source data. Source license: CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • The two hexagons stored there as pairs of coplanar quadrilaterals are represented by their six-vertex boundary cycles so the browser can triangulate each complete polygon.

Mizhaev genus-3 octahedron

  • Ruslan Mizhaev, Integer Realization of an Equivelar Octahedron of Genus 3, arXiv:2609.17700, submitted 15 September 2026. Abstract; coordinate tables.
  • The viewer transcribes the 24 integer vertex coordinates and eight cyclic nonagonal face walks from the paper's coordinate certificate.

Rost-Vigh genus-3 polyhedron

  • Gergely Rost and Viktor Vigh, A second eight-faced polyhedron in which every two faces share an edge, arXiv:2609.32998, submitted 26 September 2026. Abstract; coordinate tables.
  • The viewer transcribes the 24 rational vertex coordinates and eight nonagonal boundary cycles reported in the paper.
  • The arXiv paper is marked CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. This repository does not relicense the paper or its prose/figures; the publication is cited as the source of the mathematical coordinate and incidence data used by this independent visualization.
